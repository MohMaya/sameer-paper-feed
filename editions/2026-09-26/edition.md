## FRONT

By Riya

### Trump rejects the seven-day Hormuz offer. The midterm calendar is the fuse.

*Wall Street Journal reporting Saturday: Iran’s plan is out. A renewed bombing campaign after November is what Trump has told his staff he sees as likely.*

Iran put a seven-day path on the table through Qatari mediators: reopen the Strait of Hormuz and pause regional fighting, including Lebanon, if Washington lifts the naval blockade of Iranian ports, releases frozen funds and waives oil sanctions. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Friday at the United Nations that the countdown would begin the day the United States accepts. The Wall Street Journal reported that President Donald Trump has rejected the offer, is privately sceptical that Tehran would meet his demands, and has told aides he expects to resume bombing after the November midterms. The United States, officials told the Journal, has no intention of lifting the blockade. A U.S. official had earlier called mediated talks “positive and constructive.” Mohsen Rezaei’s four-to-five-day clock from Wednesday still points at Sunday or Monday. A diplomacy that offers barrels in seven days and prices airstrikes after an election is no longer a sequencing fight over who moves first. It is a calendar fight.

### Sensex took back 315. The barrel stayed above one hundred.

*Friday close 73,895.74 after Thursday’s −1,248. Brent eased and stayed expensive. Markets are shut Saturday.*

The Sensex rose 315.20 points, or 0.43 percent, to 73,895.74 on Friday; the Nifty gained 77.40 points, or 0.34 percent, to 23,140.50. Nineteen of thirty Sensex constituents finished higher. Economic Times Energy, citing Reuters prices into Saturday’s Asian open, had November Brent at $105.85, down 74 cents, and WTI at $93.80. Thursday’s foreign sell of ₹5,027 crore was still in the tape. A one-session clawback that leaves the barrel above one hundred and Hormuz still closed is not a relief rally. It is a pause that still prices the strait.

### IRDAI’s commission rewrite kept eating the distributors. NSE closed day two below the list price.

*First-year life commissions capped near 20–25 percent in the consultation. Feedback runs to 25 October. NSE settled ₹1,792.65.*

Indian Express’s Saturday explain put numbers on Thursday’s and Friday’s insurance wreck. PB Fintech fell about 36 percent on Thursday and another 3 percent on Friday; TurtleMint hit a 20 percent circuit on both sessions; HDFC Life and ICICI Prudential were down about 4 to 5 percent from Wednesday’s close. IRDAI’s consultation, “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution,” would cap first-year life commissions at 20 percent of premium for distributors and 25 percent for agents — less than half of what many channels now take — and cut expense-of-management limits toward 12.5 percent (life) and 20 percent (general) of gross direct premium over five years. CareEdge said twenty of twenty-two life insurers and twenty-eight of thirty-one general insurers sit above the 2028–29 EoM targets. Comments close 25 October. On the secondary tape, National Stock Exchange of India closed Friday at ₹1,792.65, down 1.39 percent from Thursday’s ₹1,818 debut settle, after a session range of ₹1,790 to ₹1,830. An exchange that lists into a barrel above one hundred and a regulator rewriting the distributor’s take is asking the secondary to do what the roadshow could not.

### Archives, tea, and a Monday trade dump. The Taiwan invoice stayed dark.

*Three-day summit closed Friday at the National Archives. USTR Greer promised more trade detail on Monday. The $14 billion arms pause did not move.*

President Donald Trump and Xi Jinping finished Friday with tea in the Red Room and a walk through the Declaration, the Constitution and the Bill of Rights. Xi framed the relationship as “strategic stability”; Trump said farmers would be happy and that the two sides had made “great strides,” without numbers. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC that details of limited trade understandings — including some agricultural and medical-device exports and consumer imports — would come out on Monday. Xi had already asked, per Xinhua, that Washington “adhere to the correct position of opposing ‘Taiwan independence.’” Trump did not adopt that formula in public. The roughly $14 billion Taiwan arms package he has held as a negotiating chip stayed paused through the Archives photograph. Allies who watched the invoice stay dark through dinner will not wait for Monday’s trade PDF to decide what the baseline now is.

## WEST ASIA — Breaking / Energy

By Aarav

### Pezeshkian offered inspectors. Houthis kept testing the Red Sea route.

*Face the Nation: inspections “absolutely” if talks open. Saturday morning: Saudi coalition said it stopped two ballistic missiles and two drones.*

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told CBS’s Face the Nation on Friday that Iran would allow United Nations nuclear inspectors if arrangements were negotiated, that nuclear weapons are “haram,” and that Tehran will not accept “bullying or coercion” while sanctions, threats and strikes continue. He said acceptance of the Hormuz proposal would start the process “from the very day.” Separately, Reuters reported early Saturday that the Saudi-led coalition intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles toward Khamis Mushait and two drones toward Riyadh, after Araqchi briefed Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan on an Iran–Oman understanding about safe Hormuz routes. Turkey and Pakistan defence officials met in Riyadh on Friday under the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement. A president who offers inspectors on camera while the Red Sea alternative is still under fire is pricing diplomacy and the workaround in the same week.

## SOUTH AMERICAS — Politics

By Aarav

### Ten days out, Datafolha still will not separate Lula from Flávio

*First round 40–36. Runoff 47–45. Margin two points. Election day is 4 October.*

Datafolha, for Folha and Globo, surveyed 2,002 people Tuesday and Wednesday and found President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva at 40 percent and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro at 36 percent in the first round, with a runoff at 47–45 — both statistical ties inside a two-point margin. A week earlier the same house had 39–36 and 46–44. Rejection is tied at 45 percent each. Lula leads among lower-income and Northeast voters; Flávio leads among evangelicals and in the South; the Southeast is a tie. Quaest on Monday had a similar deadlock. Ten days is enough time for a shock and not enough time for a durable lead. Brazil goes to the polls with the race still inside the error bar.

## USA — Politics

By Aarav

### The midterm fuse sits beside Rezaei’s clock

*Six weeks to 3 November. WSJ: Trump prices a post-election bombing campaign. Pezeshkian prices talks only after the blockade lifts.*

Thursday’s Senate refusal to force an Iran authorisation vote did not reopen Hormuz. Saturday’s Journal reporting puts the domestic and military calendars on the same page: Trump has told staff he sees renewed strikes as likely after the midterms, while insisting at the UN that election politics will not govern Iran. Pezeshkian’s Face the Nation appearance offered inspectors and refused coercion in the same breath. A chamber that will not force authorisation six weeks out and a White House that prices bombs after November are both treating the war as someone else’s immediate calendar. Rezaei’s weekend clock does not.
