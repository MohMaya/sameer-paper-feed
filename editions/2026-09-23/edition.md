## FRONT

By Riya

### Three ships, a seven-day offer, and a midterm clock

*Kpler counted three commodity vessels on Tuesday. Iran says Hormuz can reopen in a week if the blockade lifts. Brent settled Tuesday at $99.25.*

Three commodity vessels left the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, including one Panamax tanker, preliminary Kpler data showed on Wednesday. That print sat below the ten-day average of about fifteen ships. Confirmed oil transit averaged 6.98 million barrels a day in the seven days to 20 September, about 38 percent of the 18.3 million barrel pre-war baseline. Iranian crude loadings for September have so far been zero, against 893,000 barrels a day in July. In New York, President Donald Trump told the United Nations he faced a choice between a deal and annihilating the Islamic Republic, then said American envoys held a “very good” three-hour meeting with Iranian mediators. He also said he expects an agreement after November’s midterms. Tehran floated a reopening of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eases military pressure and lifts the port blockade. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s fuel, landing and ticket cutoffs on Iranian airlines take effect today. A strait that still moves oil for the world at under two-fifths of its old rate is not waiting for a midterm calendar.

### Xi lands under a tech dinner and a $14 billion pause

*Trump greets him at Andrews tonight. Thursday brings talks and an East Room state dinner. The Archives visit is Friday.*

Chinese President Xi Jinping arrives in Washington on Wednesday for his first U.S. state visit in more than a decade. President Trump will meet the aircraft at Joint Base Andrews, an unusual airport greeting, before Thursday’s White House ceremony, bilateral talks and a state dinner whose guest list includes Elon Musk, Sundar Pichai, Satya Nadella, Sam Altman, Jensen Huang, Tim Cook and Jeff Bezos. Beijing still presses a halt to Taiwan arms sales under its reading of the 1982 communiqué. A $14 billion package of missiles, anti-drone weapons and air defence remains on hold after December’s $11 billion tranche. Trump has called the arms a negotiating chip. Taiwan’s Defence Ministry said Wednesday it expects procurement to proceed as planned and ran live-fire drills with the domestically built Cheetah armoured vehicle hours before Xi’s arrival. An ally that stages a live-fire on arrival day while a dinner seats the chipmakers is an ally that has already priced the toast.

### NSE allotment is done. Listing is tomorrow.

*Demat credit and refunds land today. Grey-market premium around ₹43 implies about ₹1,828 against a ₹1,785 cap.*

National Stock Exchange of India’s ₹22,562 crore offer-for-sale allotment was finalised on Tuesday after 5.71 times overall subscription, with qualified institutional buyers at 12.68 times. Shares credit to demat accounts today. Unsuccessful bids unlock refunds the same day. Listing on the BSE is Thursday, 24 September, at the upper band of ₹1,785. The grey-market premium sat near ₹43 on Wednesday morning, a roughly 2.4 percent implied listing gain that can still move. Primary demand of that scale meets a secondary tape that spent Tuesday selling foreign money and Wednesday morning buying the oil print. An exchange that floats itself into a Hormuz week is asking the market whether infrastructure scarcity still clears when the barrel writes the morning’s risk.

*Early Wednesday the Sensex was up about 284 points. Brent traded near $98–99. Foreign institutions offloaded ₹3,810 crore on Tuesday.*

Benchmark indices rebounded in early trade on Wednesday as crude eased and Saudi Arabia restored flows on the East-West pipeline toward Yanbu. A morning that clears on pipeline restart and UN talk is still a morning that remembers how fast Hormuz rewrites the import bill.

### Pezeshkian speaks today. The airlines feel Bessent first.

*Iran’s president addresses the Assembly on Wednesday. Secondary aviation pressure begins the same calendar day.*

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian is due at the United Nations General Assembly on Wednesday after Trump’s annihilate-or-deal speech and the reported three-hour mediated exchange. Iranian conditions relayed through mediators still include lifting the naval blockade, releasing frozen assets, and ending what Tehran calls aggression across “resistance” fronts. Separately, Bessent’s secondary sanctions aimed at fuel suppliers, airports, ground handlers and ticketing firms that serve Iranian carriers take effect today. A diplomacy that prices a midterm delay while civilian aviation is cut on the same Wednesday is a diplomacy that has already chosen which channel moves first.

### Taiwan drills while the state dinner is set

*Defence ministry says the $14 billion package should proceed. The Cheetah fired on Wednesday morning.*

Hours before Xi’s arrival, Taiwan’s military conducted live-fire drills with the Cheetah, an eight-wheeled domestic armoured vehicle fitted for a 105-millimetre cannon. Maj. Gen. Chiao Fu-chun said the United States government and Congress have repeatedly backed Taiwan’s self-defence and that Taipei will keep pressing the procurement “as planned.” Washington remains bound by statute to help Taiwan defend itself even as Trump treats the paused package as leverage and Beijing cites 1982. A capital that fires its own design on summit morning is not waiting for the Archives photograph to decide what it believes about the invoice.

## SOUTH AMERICAS — Politics

By Aarav

### Lula told Turtle Bay Brazil is nobody’s backyard

*The October election is the frame. Flávio Bolsonaro remains inside a point in runoff polls.*

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva used his United Nations speech on Tuesday to warn against foreign interference in Brazil’s October election and to reject outside roles in fighting organised crime. “Brazil is nobody’s backyard,” he said, casting the vote as a choice between democracy and denialism without naming Flávio Bolsonaro, whose campaign has advertised support from Javier Milei and Benjamin Netanyahu and who met Trump earlier this year. Recent polls still have Lula and Flávio statistically tied in a hypothetical runoff. A president who opens the Assembly and still shares a point at home is a president whose foreign stage has not yet rewritten the domestic ballot.

## EUROPE — Politics / Security

By Aarav

### Schwesig builds around the AfD. NATO names the risk.

*Mecklenburg’s SPD board backed sondierung with the Left and Greens. Stringer says Putin is more cavalier.*

In Schwerin, Premier Manuela Schwesig’s SPD state board unanimously backed exploratory talks with Die Linke and the Greens after Sunday’s result left a rot-rot-grün majority of 39 seats in a 71-seat Landtag. The AfD took 38.2 percent and 32 seats but remains locked out by the firewall; the CDU’s 4.9 percent kept it out of the chamber. Chancellor Friedrich Merz still holds the federal coalition line while national AfD support sits near 30 percent. In Tallinn on Tuesday, NATO’s deputy supreme allied commander for Europe, Air Chief Marshal Sir John Stringer, told the Associated Press that Vladimir Putin is becoming “increasingly cavalier, dangerous and risk accepting” as the Ukraine war drags and hybrid probes against the alliance continue. Europe can keep a firewall as formal policy in one Landtag. It cannot keep treating the map and the eastern flank as separate files.
