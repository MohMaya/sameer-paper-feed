### Day two under the barrel. Seven hundred more off the Sensex.

*Nifty below 22,650. Brent above $107. The U.S. ten-year above 5.25%. The rupee through 96. Monthly F&O expiry.*

Indian cash markets opened the day after a 1,100-point Monday close and kept selling. Economic Times had the Sensex down nearly 700 points below 72,633 and the Nifty about 166 points below 22,650 in the Tuesday morning session. Nearly ₹4 lakh crore came off BSE market capitalisation in the first minutes, taking the listed total toward ₹474 lakh crore again. India VIX rose 5 percent to 14.37. NSE breadth was 2,258 declines against 705 advances. Bajaj Finance led the Sensex losers near 2 percent; HDFC Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Infosys, Reliance and Asian Paints each lost more than 1 percent. Brent was through $107. The U.S. ten-year cleared 5.25 percent, the thirty-year near 5.7 percent, the two-year above 4.95 percent. The rupee traded to 96.1450 — a two-month low. Monday’s FII net sale was ₹5,353 crore. September exchange outflows through last week still sit at ₹25,682 crore. Today is also September Nifty F&O expiry. Trump’s Monday denial that he had offered Iran sanctions relief or frozen funds did not cool the barrel. Mediators still sat. The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee meets 5–7 October with the market already pricing hikes. Monday’s close — Sensex 72,771.72, Nifty 22,780.25 — remains the lowest since 30 March. Hormuz progress that takes crude back under $100 is still the recovery path the tape is waiting on.

### Tata Trusts rewrite the holding company so it need not list.

*Merge TESS and TCE into Tata Sons. Target: neither NBFC nor CIC. Board and RBI still to approve.*

Eleven days after the Tata Sons board voted to pursue a listing under the RBI’s upper-layer NBFC rules, Tata Trusts — 66 percent of the company — sent the board a reorganisation plan. Merge Tata Electronics Systems Solutions and Tata Consulting Engineers into Tata Sons so the amalgamated entity fails both the NBFC principal-business test and the CIC asset test, then surrender the RBI certificate of registration. The Trusts put operating revenue of the amalgamated entity at ₹105,043 crore against ₹40,072 crore of income from financial assets — 64.3 percent operating. Group-company investments would be ₹177,120 crore of ₹200,158 crore aggregate assets, under the 90 percent CIC threshold. The plan needs the Tata Sons board and a prior RBI no-objection. Noel Tata voted against N Chandrasekaran’s five-year extension on 17 September and called that vote void. On Tuesday’s tape Tata Chemicals fell about 3.8 percent, Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles about 3 percent, Tata Investment and Tata Power more than 1.5 percent each. The listing fight has become a structure fight. The RBI still has the pen.

### BSE joins the Nifty fifty. Wipro leaves tomorrow.

*Effective 30 September. Axis Capital: about $657 million passive into BSE; about $225 million out of Wipro.*

India’s oldest exchange enters the NSE’s benchmark Nifty 50 from 30 September, replacing Wipro in the semi-annual rebalance. NSE’s August release put BSE’s six-month average free-float market capitalisation at ₹1,40,879 crore — at least 1.5 times Wipro’s ₹55,930 crore, the smallest eligible constituent. Axis Capital estimates index funds will buy about 15.7 million BSE shares (~$657 million) and sell about 114.2 million Wipro shares (~$225 million). Wipro is already down 14 percent over six months and 40 percent year to date. Macquarie initiated Outperform; Jefferies stays Underperform. The oil tape and the index tape are different bets. One clears tomorrow morning.

## INDIA — Climate / Breaking

By Aarav

### Five lakh cusecs at Valmikinagar. Bagaha broke.

*Gandak embankment breach after Nepal rains. New highest flood level at the Bagaha gauge.*

The Gandak embankment breached at Bagaha on Monday after heavy rain across the Nepal border and Bihar’s northern districts. The Water Resources Department recorded 5,43,500 cusecs at the Valmikinagar Gandak Barrage at 6 a.m. Tuesday. Discharge stayed above 500,000 cusecs for thirteen hours and above 400,000 for thirty-two. The peak was slightly under the 2024 record of 562,500 cusecs, but the Bagaha gauge set a new highest flood level. Downstream districts named on the threat list include West Champaran, East Champaran, Gopalganj, Saran, Muzaffarpur and Vaishali. West Champaran recorded two left-bank breaches; field teams secured 169 seepage and piping points. The UP rain deaths from the weekend sit under a different river system. This is the Gandak’s Tuesday number.

## SINO-ASIA — Politics / Trade

By Aarav

### Jaishankar draws the limit on a G2.

*“Limits to what the two of them can agree upon.” India–U.S. trade: more or less an understanding.*

Days after Trump and Xi met in Washington, External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar gave his first public read at the Asia Society Policy Institute in New York. The United States and China are in a competitive mode, he said. When the second-most consequential economy joins the first, “there are limits to what the two of them can agree upon vis-a-vis the rest of the world.” Everyone else is de-risking and diversifying. On the India–U.S. trade negotiation he named the hard part — small producers, small agriculture, a per-capita gap — and then the status: “Today, we have reached a situation where there is more or less an understanding on the trade agreement.” Greer’s 30-for-30 product lists still have no rates or staging dates on the page. The India–U.S. understanding Jaishankar described is a different bilateral clock. He also said that if terrorism from Pakistan — “one of my primary concerns” — is not fully appreciated by Washington, “there is an impact of that on the relationship.”

## USA — Politics

By Aarav

### Five weeks to the midterms. He denied the offer and kept the talks.

*Truth Social: “NOTHING.” Oval Office: win “very soon.” Mediators met Monday.*

President Trump on Monday called Axios’s report that he would offer Iran sanctions relief and frozen funds for nuclear progress “untrue.” “I offered them NOTHING,” he wrote, and demanded a retract. In the Oval Office the same day he said the United States would win the Iran war “very soon,” that gasoline prices would tumble, and that U.S. officials had spoken with Iranian mediators that day. More military strikes before the 3 November midterms remain “possible” in his own framing. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran had held further indirect talks through Qatari mediators in New York and hoped for Washington’s formal reply by Tuesday. Iran’s parliament speaker, Mohammad Baqer Ghalibaf, said no country in the region would export oil if Iran were blocked from selling its own. The Indian Express’s live file also carried a Wall Street Journal account that Gulf oil through Hormuz and alternative routes has recovered toward about 80 percent of pre-war flows, with Middle East crude exports near 13 million barrels a day this month. West Asia stays dark as a column. The Monday deny, the mediators’ meeting, and the midterm calendar are the Tuesday delta under Saturday’s public reject.

## EUROPE — Politics

By Aarav

### Burnham’s first conference speech as prime minister.

*Great British Grid. Social care toward the next manifesto. “Politics-as-usual has taken Britain backwards.”*

Prime Minister Andy Burnham addresses the Labour Party conference on Tuesday in his first leader’s speech since taking office, the New York Times previewed as a reset of the UK mood. Reporting ahead of the speech has him announcing a publicly owned Great British Grid aimed at bringing UK energy costs toward European levels within a decade, promising measures on youth unemployment and public control of utilities, and putting free social care in England on a path toward Labour’s next manifesto after Baroness Casey’s review — not before the next election. The conference slogan is “Hope again.” Burnham’s prepared line: hope comes from honesty and from saying the things politicians usually avoid. “We can’t carry on as we are. Politics-as-usual has taken Britain backwards.” Mecklenburg-Vorpommern’s rot-rot-grün sondierung remains a German-wire story without a fresh paid English overnight lock.
