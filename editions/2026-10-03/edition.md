## FRONT

By Riya

### Day six under the barrel. Nayara moves the pump.

*Petrol up ₹5. Diesel up ₹3. Cash still dark until Monday.*

Nayara Energy, India’s largest private fuel retailer, raised petrol by ₹5 a litre and diesel by ₹3 across 7,108 stations from the early hours of Saturday — the first clear Indian pump print of the weekend barrel. International crude and refined-product prices are squeezing margins; Icra has put oil-marketing companies at roughly ₹8 and ₹9 a litre negative marketing margins on petrol and diesel in September, and around ₹530 crore a day across petrol, diesel and LPG when retail rates stay flat. State-owned Indian Oil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum still hold more than 90 percent of India’s 1,04,137 pumps and have largely held prices; Jio-bp’s 2,304 outlets have not moved yet. The New York Times reports China has again limited refined-product exports as its own crude and product inventories dwindled — the product-side twin of Friday’s Hormuz risk premium. Indian cash, F&O, currency and commodity markets remain shut through the weekend holiday hangover and reopen Monday, 5 October. The RBI Monetary Policy Committee still meets 5–7 October. Brent’s last Friday print sat near $102; the weekend futures tape is thin. The next settle that matters is Monday’s open.

### Three FIRs after Jantar. The SIR map still moves north.

*Parliament Street and Connaught Place. Uttarakhand’s final roll is out — and short.*

Delhi Police registered three FIRs on Saturday against unidentified people over Friday’s blocked attempt to gather at Jantar Mantar against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar — two at Parliament Street and one at Connaught Place, under Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita sections on disobedience, obstruction, criminal force against a public servant, and common intention. Friday’s detention count in the capital landed around 550 in the Express’s Saturday write-up; Mumbai’s Cockroach Janta Party demonstration at Shivaji Park went ahead the same day. Farther north, Uttarakhand published its final Special Intensive Revision roll on Saturday with 71,58,949 electors — about 8.02 lakh below the 79,60,762 on the roll when SIR began on 8 June. The Election Commission has ordered Booth Level Officers to compare pre-SIR and final lists, visit missing electors at home, and facilitate Form 6 enrolment, aiming for roughly 20–25 identified electors a week so the drive can finish in about a month. The street FIRs and the roll gap are the same SIR week in two registers.

## WEST ASIA — Security / Aviation

By Aarav

### The crash axe was in the cockpit. Emirates calls it terror.

*Co-pilot used the axe on Captain Smit Machchhar. FlyDubai to Tel Aviv.*

Emirates authorities say the FlyDubai co-pilot used a cockpit crash axe in what they are treating as a terrorist midair assault on the captain of a flight bound for Tel Aviv — a Saturday confirmation that hardens the earlier stabbing timeline. The New York Times reports the attack has deepened concerns about the safety of Israelis traveling abroad; Captain Smit Machchhar, badly wounded, managed to unlock the cockpit door so passengers and crew could overwhelm the attacker and divert the Boeing 737 into Saudi Arabia. The Front owns the barrel and the China product curb. This desk is the aviation delta, not a second oil column.

## USA — Economy / Politics

By Aarav

### The White House calls it public relations. The polls call it the wallet.

*Midterms are 3 November. Economic approval is the campaign he did not want.*

President Trump told reporters the economy has a “public relations” problem — that the country is “doing better than any country in the world economically” and that what is missing is the explanation. The New York Times frames the same week as a president who hoped to tout growth on the trail and is instead fighting high prices and slow hiring with five weeks to the midterms. An AP-NORC poll circulating with the coverage puts approval of his economic handling near 26 percent and cost-of-living handling near 17 percent; about 65 percent blame his policies for persistently high costs. Diesel near $6.39 a gallon was Friday’s household print of the barrel. The campaign clock that is not Hormuz is 3 November.

## SOUTH AMERICAS — Politics

By Aarav

### Brazil votes tomorrow. Lula against another Bolsonaro.

*First round Sunday 4 October. Runoff 25 October if needed. Four Supreme Court seats on the table.*

Around 160 million Brazilians can vote Sunday in a general election that will choose a president, governors, two-thirds of the Senate and the entire lower house. The Economist’s tracker has the race as Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers’ Party against Flávio Bolsonaro, a Liberal Party senator and son of Jair Bolsonaro, who remains under house arrest for the post-2022 coup attempt. If nobody clears half the ballots, a runoff follows on 25 October. The next president can appoint four of eleven Supreme Court justices. The Tracker also notes Trump’s tariff pressure on Brazilian imports and a streak of right-wing wins across Latin America since January 2025. The question Sunday answers is whether the first round ends or merely narrows.

## EUROPE — Diplomacy / Energy

By Aarav

### Putin put Lukoil on the Ukraine table. The envoys took notes.

*Fields, refineries, New Jersey pumps. About $20 billion. Treasury still holds the pen.*

President Vladimir Putin raised a sale of Lukoil’s international energy assets with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, President Trump’s envoys, during September talks that were supposed to be about ending the war in Ukraine, The New York Times reports. The portfolio — oil fields, refineries in Europe that make diesel and jet fuel, and gas stations including in New Jersey — was valued by Lukoil at about $20 billion earlier this year. A leading bid group includes Todd Boehly and Middle Eastern investors with prior business ties to the Trump circle; the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation has sought a stake. There is no indication Kushner or Witkoff personally profit, the Times says, even as ethics advisers call the entanglements alarming. Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control still must approve; the latest sanctions deadline extension runs through 29 October. This is Russian diesel and a peace channel — not the Hormuz spine on Front.
