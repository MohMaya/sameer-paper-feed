## FRONT

By Riya

### Trump said the quiet part on camera. Hormuz stays closed.

*Saturday at the White House he rejected Iran’s seven-day plan in his own voice. The Journal’s midterm bombing baseline did not move.*

President Donald Trump told reporters on Saturday that he had rejected Tehran’s proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and pause regional fighting within seven days. “I reject their proposal,” he said before leaving for Tennessee. “I rejected their deal.” He cast Iran as desperate for the waterway because “they're losing so badly” and “have no money coming in,” and said the United States was “winning tremendously.” The Wall Street Journal had already reported, citing unnamed officials, that Trump was privately sceptical Iran would meet his terms, that Washington had no intention of lifting the naval blockade, and that he had told aides he sees a renewed bombing campaign as likely after the November midterms. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi had put the seven-day countdown on the table Friday at the United Nations through Qatari mediators — reopen Hormuz and pause fighting, including Lebanon, if the blockade, frozen funds and oil sanctions move. A plan that lived overnight as Journal attribution is now a public no. The midterm calendar is still the fuse the Journal named.

### Drought in three-quarters of Maharashtra. The cash is in the next GR.

*265 of 358 talukas. Concessions today. Crop-loss money after panchnama.*

Maharashtra’s Government Resolution put 265 of 358 talukas under drought — about three-quarters of the state. The order lists land-revenue exemption, crop-loan restructuring, a stay on recovery, agricultural power concessions, exam-fee waivers, looser employment-guarantee norms, tankers, fodder and drinking water. What it does not do is put interim crop-loss cash in farmers’ accounts. That payment is deferred to a separate resolution after field panchnamas. NCP (SP) leader Mahesh Tapase said Sharad Pawar’s 16 September letter had already asked for at least ₹50,000 a hectare and that farmers “cannot survive on promises of a future GR.” Shiv Sena (UBT) MLA Aaditya Thackeray said the declaration should have come earlier and that relief must clear into bank accounts, including unpaid crop-insurance claims. A drought order that rates the state and postpones the cheque is asking the survey to do the politics the GR would not.

### SIR notices will come to the door. The claims clock moved to 12 October.

*Full Commission, Saturday. BLOs collect documents at home. Old 30 September deadline is gone.*

The Election Commission of India eased the Special Intensive Revision notice process after Maharashtra’s chief electoral officer wrote on 24 September. Voters flagged as unmapped or carrying logical discrepancies will not ordinarily be summoned to the registration office. Booth Level Officers are to visit homes, take documents and upload them on ECINET for the electoral registration officer to decide. Hearings become the exception, preferably online, with an adult family member allowed to appear. The Commission also pushed Maharashtra’s deadline for claims and objections to 12 October 2026 and set 10 November for disposing of notices and claims. Saturday’s Brief still carried claims to 30 September. A revision that softens the hearing and lengthens the clock is answering the CEO’s inconvenience argument without answering the Bombay High Court petitions that want deleted voters restored.

[Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/mumbai/eci-eases-sir-notice-rules-blo-doorstep-document-collection-maharashtra-10895664/)

### Thirteen million barrels and a refining story. The barrel still cleared above one hundred.

*Energy Secretary Wright: almost 13 mb/d on average; one day above 20. Friday’s Brent settle was $104.32.*

U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Fox News that oil moving through the Strait of Hormuz was averaging almost 13 million barrels a day, with one day last week above 20 million — more than pre-conflict levels. “The pricing problem today is much more refining capacity than it actually is the flow of oil,” he said. CNBC’s Friday settle had Brent at $104.32, down 2.1 percent on the day and flat on the week, and West Texas Intermediate at $92.41, down 2.3 percent on the day and 7.9 percent on the week. Economic Times Energy’s Friday Asian print had still shown Brent near $105.85 into the session. Indian equity markets are shut until Monday. A White House that rejects Tehran’s price for a full reopening while its energy secretary advertises recovered flows is telling the barrel that escort capacity, not a Qatari seven-day deal, is the near-term policy.

## WEST ASIA — Breaking / Energy

By Aarav

### The public no lands on Rezaei’s weekend clock

*Trump’s Saturday gaggle. Araqchi’s seven-day offer still priced on U.S. acceptance. Midterms on 3 November.*

Saturday’s Brief carried the Journal’s account that Trump had rejected Iran’s mediated plan and priced bombs after the midterms. By Saturday afternoon he had said it himself on camera. Araqchi’s UN framing did not change overnight: acceptance in Washington starts the seven-day countdown; without it, Hormuz and the regional pause stay closed. Pezeshkian’s Friday offer of inspectors if talks open, and his refusal of coercion, still sits beside a president who will not lift the blockade first. Wright’s flow numbers, if they hold, weaken Tehran’s claim that the strait is an on-off veto and strengthen Washington’s preference to wait past 3 November. The uncomfortable question is no longer who speaks first through Doha. It is whether a recovered barrel average makes a post-election bombing campaign cheaper to threaten than a blockade waiver is to grant.

## USA — Politics

By Aarav

### Five and a half weeks to the midterms. The war is on the same page now.

*3 November. Public reject on Saturday. Journal baseline: bombs after the election.*

Thursday’s Senate refusal to force an Iran authorisation vote did not reopen Hormuz. Saturday’s public rejection puts the domestic and military calendars in one frame the Journal had already sketched in private. Trump insists he likes deals and will not let Iran have a nuclear weapon; he also told staff, per the Journal, that he sees renewed strikes as likely once the midterms are done. A chamber that will not force authorisation and a White House that prices the next air campaign after voters speak are both treating the war as someone else’s immediate week. Rezaei’s clock, and Araqchi’s seven days from acceptance, do not.
