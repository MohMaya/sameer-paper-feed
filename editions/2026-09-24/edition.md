### NSE lists at ₹1,800. The tape does the rest.

*Debut on the BSE at a 0.84 percent premium. By mid-morning Moneycontrol had it at ₹1,878. Market capitalisation about ₹4.45 lakh crore against BSE’s ₹1.33 lakh crore.*

National Stock Exchange of India listed on the BSE on Thursday at ₹1,800, 0.84 percent above the ₹1,785 IPO price, after a ₹22,569 crore offer-for-sale that closed 5.71 times subscribed and 12.68 times with qualified institutional buyers. At 10:51 a.m. Indian Express had the stock at ₹1,860.10, up 3.3 percent from the list price. Moneycontrol later printed a session high near ₹1,878, a 5.21 percent gain. Listing-day market capitalisation sat at about ₹4.45 lakh crore, more than three times BSE’s ₹1.33 lakh crore. The shareholder register is said to have widened from roughly two lakh names to about thirty-six lakh. An exchange that floats itself into a morning when Brent sits above one hundred dollars is asking whether infrastructure scarcity still clears when the barrel writes the risk.

### Bessent’s barrels meet Rezaei’s four-to-five-day clock

*The Treasury secretary told Fox News the Navy is moving 10, 15, sometimes 17 million barrels a day through Hormuz. Iran’s security chief gave Washington days, not weeks.*

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Wednesday that American forces were helping get between 10 million and 17 million barrels of oil a day out of the Strait of Hormuz despite drone attacks on tankers. Pre-war transit was about 20 million barrels a day, he said, with roughly 3 million now on alternative routes. Separately, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, told state television that the Americans have four to five days to accept Iran’s seven conditions. Until then, he said, there will be no negotiations and Hormuz will not reopen. Associated Press reporting on Gulf workarounds still puts the dark southern shuttle at about 6 million barrels a day or more, with Fujairah adding about 2 million. A strait that Washington counts in the teens and Tehran still treats as closed is not a midterm calendar. It is a sequence dispute with a short fuse.

### Xi’s dinner day opens with a two-month trade truce

*Trump’s rare Andrews welcome was Wednesday. Thursday is the Oval and the East Room. Friday is tea and the Archives.*

President Donald Trump personally greeted Xi Jinping at Joint Base Andrews on Wednesday, a rare airport welcome that opened Xi’s first U.S. state visit in more than a decade. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent then said the U.S.-China trade truce that had been due to expire on 10 November would run to 10 January 2027 after talks with Vice Premier He Lifeng. Thursday’s programme is the formal White House welcome, Oval Office and Cabinet Room talks, and an East Room state banquet whose American guest list still includes the big tech chief executives; AFP sources said Xi’s delegation brought no Chinese CEOs. Friday closes with tea at the White House and a visit to the National Archives. Trump confirmed Iran is on the agenda. A summit that extends the detente by sixty days before the first formal toast is a summit that has already priced the photograph.

### Sensex gives back Wednesday. PB Fintech hits the rail.

*Early Thursday the Sensex was down 632.68 points. Brent traded near $102.2. FIIs bought ₹1,617.45 crore on Wednesday.*

The Sensex fell 632.68 points to 74,189.55 in early trade on Thursday; the Nifty dropped 216.15 points to 23,222.55. Brent was quoted around $102.2 a barrel. The U.S. ten-year yield sat near 5.11 percent. Wednesday’s close had been a rebound — Sensex up 299.17 points to 74,828.25, Nifty up 117.80 to 23,446.80 — after foreign institutions bought ₹1,617.45 crore of Indian equities on the exchange print. Insurance names led the Thursday sell-off after IRDAI floated an overhaul of expense-of-management limits and commission caps; PB Fintech fell as much as 26 percent, and life insurers were down as much as 6 percent. A listing morning that opens with the barrel above one hundred and a regulator rewriting the distributor’s take is not a quiet debut tape.

## WEST ASIA — Breaking / Energy

By Aarav

### Pezeshkian held the photographs. Tehran argued over the contact.

*Iran’s president addressed the Assembly on Wednesday. The Araghchi–Witkoff channel is the domestic fight.*

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told the United Nations General Assembly on Wednesday that he had rewritten his speech after Donald Trump’s annihilate-or-deal address, held up photographs of children killed in the Minab school strike, and said Iran remained open to diplomacy without “the language of coercion.” The same day, hardline outlets attacked Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi over a Tuesday contact with U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff that U.S. officials have described as face-to-face and Iranian accounts insist was meant only to convey seven conditions through mediators. Bessent’s secondary aviation pressure — fuel, landing and ticket cutoffs aimed at firms that serve Iranian carriers — was already biting; flights to Baghdad and Muscat were among those cancelled. A diplomacy that opens Turtle Bay with children’s photographs and closes the same calendar day arguing over who was allowed to speak to Witkoff is a diplomacy that has not yet settled who can sign.

## SINO-ASIA — Politics

By Aarav

### The Archives is Friday. The arms package is still the invoice.

*Taiwan’s $14 billion pause and Wednesday’s Cheetah drills remain the ally’s calendar against the summit’s.*

Thursday’s White House programme seats American chip and platform chiefs at the state dinner while Beijing continues to press a halt to Taiwan arms sales under its reading of the 1982 communiqué. The $14 billion package of missiles, anti-drone weapons and air defence stays on hold after December’s $11 billion tranche. Trump has called the arms a negotiating chip. Taiwan’s Defence Ministry said on Wednesday it expects procurement to proceed as planned and ran live-fire drills with the domestically built Cheetah hours before Xi’s arrival. An ally that fired its own design on arrival day is not waiting for Friday’s Archives photograph to decide what it believes about the invoice.

## EUROPE — Politics

By Aarav

### Schwesig’s sondierung starts next week

*Rot-rot-grün arithmetic is 39 seats of 71. The AfD’s 38.2 percent stays outside the room.*

In Schwerin, Premier Manuela Schwesig plans to open exploratory talks with Die Linke and the Greens in the week of 28 September after Sunday’s result left a rot-rot-grün majority of 39 seats in a 71-seat Landtag. The AfD took 38.2 percent and 32 seats; the SPD took 35.5 percent and 29. The firewall still locks the AfD out. Chancellor Friedrich Merz holds the federal coalition line while national AfD support sits near 30 percent. A Landtag that can build around the first-place party without inviting it is still a Landtag that has to govern beside a map the centre did not draw.
