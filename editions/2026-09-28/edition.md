## FRONT

By Riya

### Sensex loses eleven hundred. The barrel and the bond did it.

*Brent near $106–107. The U.S. ten-year above 5.2%. Nearly ₹8 lakh crore off BSE market capitalisation.*

Indian cash markets reopened into a broad sell-off. The Sensex fell more than 1,100 points to trade below 72,800. The Nifty 50 dropped more than 350 points below 22,800. Economic Times put the hit to BSE-listed market capitalisation near ₹8 lakh crore, taking the total toward ₹474 lakh crore. All thirty Sensex constituents traded red. Bajaj Finance, Kotak Mahindra Bank and HDFC Bank led the slide near 2 percent each. India VIX jumped nearly 13 percent toward 14. NSE breadth was 2,259 declines against 759 advances. The same tape named the stack: Trump’s Saturday rejection of Iran’s Hormuz plan, oil back through $106, the U.S. ten-year above 5.2 percent — its highest since 2004 — the rupee 14 paise softer at 95.89, and Friday’s FII net sale of ₹3,694 crore. September FPI outflows through exchanges have already touched ₹25,682 crore. Asian peers were weak with it. Kospi fell more than 2 percent. Shanghai Composite fell more than 1.7 percent. Axis Direct’s tape note, via Economic Times, kept the Nifty bias neutral-to-cautious below 23,300 and named Tuesday’s September series expiry as a volatility date. Hormuz progress that cools crude is still the recovery path they wrote down. Until then the reopen is pricing the war and the Treasury market, not domestic earnings.

### Bombay High Court set two SIR clocks. Neither is today.

*Kolhapur bench: 22 October. Principal seat: 5 October. Urgent listing refused on both.*

The Bombay High Court’s Kolhapur circuit bench on Monday put a writ petition by four Maharashtra voters over to 22 October. The plea, citing The Indian Express’s investigation into Special Intensive Revision, asks the Election Commission to place fourteen “unilateral administrative orders” on record, restore deleted voters, quash SIR directions for the state, and hold Assembly elections until rolls are approved by a statutory majority of the multi-member Commission. Justices Sharmila U Deshmukh and Neeraj P Dhote asked what private right the petitioners held against the Commission and why production was sought when notices were already out. “How is the production sought when notices are issued? We will see that on October 22,” the bench said. At the principal seat, Chief Justice Mahesh Chandra Tripathi listed a separate writ on document collection and “uncollectible” exclusions for 5 October after advocate Prakash Ambedkar argued nearly two crore voters face deletion. Saturday’s Brief had the Commission easing doorstep notices and moving Maharashtra’s claims deadline to 12 October. Swarajya’s Sunday account of the full-bench meeting carried the Commission’s line that nationwide SIR decisions were unanimous and that Form 6’s SIR declaration had already been upheld by the Supreme Court. Monday’s courts did not accelerate the fight. They dated it.

### Oil back through one hundred and six. Talks are still on the week.

*Brent +$1.65 to $106. WTI +99¢ to $93.40. Kpler: Middle East crude exports 12.8 mb/d in September.*

Brent crude futures rose $1.65, or 1.5 percent, to $106 a barrel on Monday. West Texas Intermediate was at $93.40, up 99 cents. Economic Times tied the rebound to Trump’s Saturday rejection of Iran’s mediated plan to reopen Hormuz. In a Sunday phone interview with Axios, Trump said he still expected U.S. negotiators to hold further talks this week even after the public no. Preliminary Kpler data on Monday put crude exports from key Middle East producers at 12.8 million barrels a day in September — the highest since the war began in February — with Hormuz shipments on track for about 7.4 million barrels a day this month. Goldman’s Daan Struyven told Bloomberg shipping risk could take Brent toward $120 if attacks intensify, or back toward $80 if exports normalize. JPMorgan said it no longer has a clear baseline scenario six months into the war. West Asia stays dark today. The Monday price and the week’s talks calendar are the delta under Saturday’s reject.

## INDIA — Markets

By Aarav

### Policybazaar’s parent put the consultation on the exchange tape.

*Clarification Saturday. Brokerages still model 30 to 50 percent revenue damage if the draft holds. Comments to 25 October.*

PB Fintech told the exchanges on Saturday that IRDAI’s consultation paper on distribution expenses and commission caps could affect the economics of online insurance sale, and that the recent price and volume moves reflected market dynamics and sector news rather than a company-specific disclosure. The paper remains consultation, not an order. New Indian Express carried the company’s line that proposed limits may not fully reflect operating and servicing costs of online distribution. Swarajya’s read of the same draft put the regulator’s own cost complaint on the page: payouts to some corporate agents up 125 percent between FY23 and FY25 while their premium grew 28 percent, with distribution already consuming roughly 27 percent of first-year life premiums. The consultation also proposes cutting life expenses of management toward 12.5 percent of premium in five years and general toward 20 percent, banning insurance as a mandatory loan condition, and clawing back commission on proven mis-selling.

Motilal Oswal, via Economic Times BFSI, modeled a potential 30 percent hit to core online insurance revenue in FY28 if the draft lands as written, with general-insurance NPV cut 60–65 percent under product-wise caps and life NPV nearer flat. Management expects no FY27 hit, possible cost cuts of 10–15 percent on a roughly ₹30 billion base, and volume offsets of 15–20 percent if lower commissions pass into prices. Brokerages outside that note have floated 35–50 percent revenue damage over a couple of years. Comments still close 25 October. The Sensex tape on Monday was about oil and yields. The IRDAI clock is still a separate bet.

## SINO-ASIA — Business / Trade

By Aarav

### Managed trade now has an annex. It does not have a tariff schedule.

*Board of Trade recommendations public Monday. Seventy-seven Chinese lines. Sixteen hundred American. Truce already pointed at 10 January 2027.*

Friday’s Brief carried Greer’s promise that product-level detail on the nonsensitive basket would come Monday. Monday delivered lists, not rates. Greer’s USTR statement framed the recommendations as about $30 billion each way under the Board of Trade Trump and Xi stood up in Beijing in May and pressed again in Washington last week — “about 30 percent of U.S. exports to China” on the American side if farm goods and medical devices move, and household goods and toys the United States “generally does not import from other countries” on the Chinese side. Economic Times had already tracked the $30 billion managed-trade frame through the summit week. Swarajya’s Saturday read of Beijing’s eight-point consensus had put the reciprocal $30 billion cut, an AI dialogue with a November round, and an incident channel on the same page as the truce extension Bessent had already dated toward January.

What Monday adds is the shape of the ask. Reporting on the published annexes counts 77 Chinese product lines into the United States — toys, tableware, Christmas ornaments, microwave ovens, highchairs, sports balls — against 1,619 American lines into China, heavy on livestock, frozen meat, seafood, soy, whiskey and seed. What it does not add is the percentage cut, the staging, or a date before the holiday season retailers want. The Busan-era truce extension toward mid-January is the only dated ceiling on the page. A Board that can publish sixteen hundred lines and still withhold the duty table is telling exporters which politics happens in the annex and which still needs a quarter.

## USA — Politics

By Aarav

### Five weeks to the midterms. The week’s talks sit under the same reject.

*3 November. Axios Sunday: Witkoff and Kushner still expected at the table. Public no unchanged.*

Trump’s Saturday gaggle rejected Iran’s seven-day Hormuz plan in his own voice. On Sunday he told Axios he still expected further talks this week with U.S. envoys, including Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, working through Qatari mediators. The Wall Street Journal’s baseline — renewed strikes more likely after the midterms — did not get a Monday rewrite. Five weeks remain to 3 November. A president who can reject the seven-day clock on camera and still schedule negotiators for the same week is running two calendars in one sentence. The force extensions Hegseth has already priced into 2027 sit under both.
