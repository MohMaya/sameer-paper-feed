### Day three under the barrel. Sensex lifts. Nifty does not.

*Oil cools below $104, then climbs again. The U.S. thirty-year above 5.61%. Four-day FII sales near ₹24,000 crore.*

Indian cash markets opened Wednesday split. Economic Times had the Sensex up about 130 points above 72,660 and the Nifty about 20 points below 22,700 around 10 a.m. Breadth flipped positive — 2,186 advances against 888 declines on the NSE. TCS, ICICI Bank, Tech Mahindra, IndiGo and Kotak Mahindra Bank led the Sensex gainers; Adani Ports, Sun Pharma and HDFC Bank slipped. Oil had eased below $104 after Middle East crude exports recovered to 16.328 million barrels a day in September, then climbed again when Trump’s Monday denial that he had offered Iran sanctions relief was still the overnight frame. Brent futures were up $1.14 to $103.73; West Texas Intermediate near $89.50–$89.72. Brent is on track for a monthly gain of about 14 percent. The U.S. thirty-year yield cleared 5.61 percent on Tuesday — the highest since 2002. FIIs sold ₹24,054 crore of equity in the last four sessions. Nifty’s September correction sits near 5.67 percent. Qatar said Tuesday it was still exchanging messages between Washington and Tehran. The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee meets 5–7 October. Hormuz progress that takes crude back under $100 remains the tape’s recovery path.

### BSE joins the fifty. The stock falls four percent.

*Goldman and BNP sell more than 68 lakh shares. Nuvama still sees about $668 million of passive inflows.*

BSE Ltd entered the Nifty 50 on Wednesday, replacing Wipro in the semi-annual rebalance, and the stock dropped about 4 percent on the debut session — to a morning print near ₹3,085. Economic Times tied the slide to bulk deals: Goldman Sachs Investments Mauritius sold 23.8 lakh shares at ₹3,200 and BNP Paribas Financial Markets sold 44.52 lakh at a weighted average near ₹3,199.54 — more than 68.33 lakh shares, about 1.67 percent of equity, for roughly ₹2,186 crore. UTI Mutual Fund and Nippon India Mutual Fund bought about 52.43 lakh shares for roughly ₹1,678 crore. Nuvama Institutional Equities still puts expected Nifty-50 passive inflows into BSE near $668 million. The oil tape and the index tape remain different bets. One cleared this morning.

### Noel Tata asks the RBI to sit with the Trusts’ plan.

*“Back to our roots.” Board and RBI still to act on the TESS–TCE merger into Tata Sons.*

A day after Tata Trusts sent Tata Sons a reorganisation plan to dodge an RBI-mandated listing, Trusts chairman Noel Tata made the public ask. At a Republic TV event he said the proposal — merge Tata Electronics Systems Solutions and Tata Consulting Engineers into Tata Sons so the amalgamated entity is neither NBFC nor CIC — “fits within the existing guidelines of the Reserve Bank.” “I hope the RBI will engage with us on this subject and we should be able to find some common ground and, hopefully, a solution which avoids us from listing,” he said. “It really is a solution. It’s not rocket science. It’s a solution that takes us back to our roots.” The Trusts hold 66 percent of Tata Sons. The Tata Sons board and a prior RBI no-objection are still owed. The listing fight remains a structure fight.

## INDIA — Business / Energy

By Aarav

### Cut the import dependence. The basket is already at $120.

*Shaktikanta Das on Samudra Manthan. Graham Act tariff risk. Russia still near half of India’s crude.*

Principal Secretary-2 to the Prime Minister Shaktikanta Das said on Tuesday that India must reduce its dependence on imported energy. India meets more than 88 percent of its crude needs from abroad; Russia currently supplies nearly half of those imports. Das pointed to the offshore exploration scheme Samudra Manthan — first-phase outlay ₹84,084 crore through 2030–31 — as a “strong impetus” to domestic hydrocarbon production. The comments land after the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 gave President Trump power to levy up to 100 percent tariffs on countries that keep buying Russian oil and gas. India’s crude basket was $120.8 a barrel on Monday. Public-sector oil marketing companies still face daily retail-fuel losses of more than ₹500 crore. The Busan truce between Washington and Beijing runs toward 10 January 2027. India’s Russian cargoes sit outside that bilateral clock.

## SINO-ASIA — Politics

By Aarav

### Jaishankar names the food crisis the G2 will not fix.

*Black Sea grain. West Asian fertiliser. “Major food crisis” in the coming months.*

At the same Asia Society conversation in New York where he drew limits on a G2, External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar warned that the Ukraine war and the Iran–Gulf conflict could trigger a “major food crisis” in the coming months. Some major grain exporters, he said, may not be able to send shipments — “particularly out to the Black Sea.” Fertiliser production in West Asia and Russia has been hit; countries around Hormuz supply about 13 percent of global nitrogen and 9 percent of phosphate fertiliser exports. The FAO Food Price Index for August sat at 133.3 — highest since November 2022. Combined with energy tightness and capital flight to safe harbours, the pressure on Global South economies is “very very stressful,” he said. Monday’s G2 limit was the diplomatic sentence. The food sentence is the harvest calendar.

## EUROPE — Politics

By Aarav

### The Eiffel Tower’s chief leaves after the BAPS visit.

*SETE probe: operational failures. Female staff excluded on 5 September. Ruivo out by year-end.*

Patrick Branco Ruivo, director general of Société d’Exploitation de la tour Eiffel since 2018, will leave at the end of 2026 after an investigation into a 5 September visit by a Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha (BAPS) delegation. SETE said the probe found “operational flaws and shortcomings” that produced the “unacceptable” exclusion of female staff. The CGT union said women were sidelined and replaced. Staff protests closed the tower on 7 September. BAPS said the arrangement was “never… rooted in gender” and apologised for inconvenience; the group was in France to inaugurate a temple in Bussy-Saint-Georges on 6 September. SETE chair Ariel Weil called for a protocol department and gender-equality training. Burnham’s Liverpool speech remains Tuesday’s Europe tape without a fresh paid overnight lock beyond the New York Times preview.
