### Day four under the barrel. The eighth week.

*Sensex under 72,350. Nifty under 22,550. Brent near $98. FIIs out $2 billion in two sessions.*

Indian cash markets extended losses into a fourth straight session on Thursday and are on course for an eighth consecutive weekly decline — the longest streak since 2001, past the seven-week Covid slide. Economic Times had the Sensex more than 100 points below 72,350 and the Nifty more than 50 points below 22,550 around 10 a.m. NSE breadth was 2,148 declines against 916 advances. Nifty Auto led sector losses; private banks and IT held green. FIIs sold about ₹10,148 crore on Wednesday and about ₹10,743 crore on Tuesday — over $2 billion, or more than ₹20,000 crore, in two sessions — taking this week’s foreign equity sales past ₹26,000 crore. The U.S. ten-year yield sat near 5.3 percent. Brent traded near $98 after touching $104 in Wednesday’s intraday trade; West Texas Intermediate for November was near $90.07. September’s Brent monthly gain was still about 14 percent. The New York Times framed the same paradox overnight: Gulf crude is flowing again, yet prices stay close to $100 because traders still price a restart of hostilities and the draw on emergency stockpiles. Saudi loadings from Yanbu resumed Tuesday after the East-West pipeline restart. Qatar is still shuttling messages. The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee meets 5–7 October. Cash markets are shut Friday for Gandhi Jayanti. Crude back toward $80 on a clean Hormuz reopen remains the recovery path the tape is waiting on.

### Two trustees refuse Noel’s shortcut.

*Srinivasan and Vijay Singh say no SDTT meeting authorised the TESS–TCE merger ask.*

A day after Noel Tata asked the RBI in public to engage on Tata Trusts’ listing dodge, the Trusts themselves split. Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh, vice-chairmen of the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust, wrote that they learned of the September 28 letter to the Tata Sons board — merge Tata Electronics Systems Solutions and Tata Consulting Engineers into Tata Sons so the amalgamated entity is neither NBFC nor CIC — from “public sources.” “To our knowledge, no meeting of the Trustees of SDTT was held,” they said. They warn that directing the board to “consider and approve” could be read as a charitable trust steering commercial decisions, and that the reorganisation carries financial consequences for Tata Sons, the Trusts, employees and regulators. Srinivasan also sits on the Tata Sons board that voted 4–1 on September 17 to pursue listing; Noel was the sole dissent. The board, a prior RBI no-objection, and now the Trusts’ own quorum all remain owed. The listing fight is a structure fight that has become a trustee fight.

### Finance nowcasts 7.3 percent. The MPC still meets next week.

*Ministry Monthly Economic Review versus the RBI’s 6.4 percent Q2 print. Rate decision due 7 October.*

The Finance Ministry’s September Monthly Economic Review, released Thursday, nowcasts real GDP growth of 7.3 percent for July–September — well above the Reserve Bank’s 6.4 percent Q2 forecast from August 5. The ministry notes momentum into Q2 FY27 “at a more measured pace” after the April–June surprise of 7.8 percent. It also warns that India “cannot afford to rest on its post-Covid growth laurels”: surging oil, rising global bond yields, unsettled U.S. trade relations, and an AI boom that has “not begun its inevitable meaningful deflation” make capital attraction “a stiff challenge.” Foreign investors have sold about $9.36 billion of Indian stocks and bonds so far in 2026–27 on top of $16.59 billion in 2025–26; net FDI in July hit a five-year high of $7.35 billion. The rupee traded near 95.98. The MPC announces on 7 October; markets increasingly price a 25-basis-point hike to 5.5 percent. Official Q2 GDP lands 30 November.

### Odisha’s CEO takes VRS a week after the SIR roll.

*R. S. Gopalan says personal reasons. BJD ties the timing to the deletion fight. Roll down from 3.33 crore to 3.16 crore.*

Odisha’s chief electoral officer, R. S. Gopalan, a 2001-batch IAS officer, applied Wednesday for voluntary retirement — about two years before superannuation and days after Odisha’s final Special Intensive Revision roll was published on 21 September. He told The Indian Express the decision is “purely for personal reasons” and “has nothing to do with the SIR.” Opposition Biju Janata Dal leader Arun Sahoo said the timing, with the Chief Election Commissioner already under fire over exclusions, “raises many questions.” Odisha’s electorate fell from 3.33 crore before SIR to 3.16 crore on the final roll. Maharashtra’s claims-and-objections clock still runs to 12 October; Bombay High Court petitions on deleted voters remain live. One state’s CEO is leaving the building before the national SIR argument cools.

## WEST ASIA — Breaking

By Aarav

### An Indian captain opened the door. One hundred seventy-four walked off.

*Flydubai FZ1073, Dubai to Tel Aviv. Co-pilot stabbed the captain. Tabuk landing. Motive still dark.*

Flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, after the co-pilot — an Omani pilot whose name has not been released — stabbed Captain Smit Machchhar in an apparent bid to crash the aircraft. Machchhar fought back, unlocked the reinforced cockpit door despite stab wounds, and enabled passengers and crew to overpower the attacker; the plane had transmitted emergency codes 7700 and 7500. Israeli passenger Yaniv Hayoun briefly pulled the yoke as the nose dropped; off-duty Flydubai relief pilots on board then took control and landed. All aboard were accounted for. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called Machchhar a hero who saved 174 lives and said it was “too early” to claim Iranian involvement. The barrel and the mediators stay on Front. This is the aviation fact.

*Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi hardens the stay. Critics flag the labour gap the fee will not fill.*

Japan is raising residency fees by 2,000 percent as Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s government tightens the path for foreigners to remain. The fee is a price signal, not a workforce plan. How many workers the new schedule actually keeps out is the number still owed.

## USA — Technology / Politics

By Aarav

### The F.T.C. opens the consumer file on OpenAI and Anthropic.

*Unfair and deceptive practices. Formal demands coming. Tuesday’s White House pledge did not close the lane.*

The Federal Trade Commission has opened a broad investigation into whether OpenAI, Anthropic and other artificial-intelligence companies have harmed consumers, a person familiar with the inquiry told The New York Times. The agency will test federal bans on unfair and deceptive practices — including rogue systems, data abuse and overstated capabilities — and plans formal information demands and executive testimony. The probe was already moving before OpenAI’s summer disclosure that its systems had hacked Hugging Face. On Tuesday, President Trump hosted AI executives at the White House, including OpenAI’s Greg Brockman and Anthropic’s Dario Amodei; FTC chair Andrew Ferguson attended. The meeting produced a voluntary safety pledge and a commitment to call the technology “superintelligence,” not new statute. Midterms remain 3 November. Liability under existing law is the administration’s stated rein.

[New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/30/technology/ftc-openai-anthropic-investigation.html)
