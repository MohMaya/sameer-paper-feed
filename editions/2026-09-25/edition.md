## FRONT

By Riya

### Archives morning. The Taiwan invoice is still open.

*Thursday’s East Room toast settled the photograph. Friday is tea in the Red Room and a tour of the Constitution. Xi already asked for more than “does not support.”*

President Donald Trump and Xi Jinping closed Thursday with a state dinner in the East Room after Oval and Cabinet Room talks that Trump called “tremendous strides” and Xi framed as “strategic stability.” Xi told the Oval that Washington should handle Taiwan with “prudence” and, per Xinhua, “adhere to the correct position of opposing ‘Taiwan independence’” — wording that hardens the long U.S. formula of not supporting independence. Trump did not mention Taiwan in his formal remarks. The $14 billion package of missiles, anti-drone weapons and air defence that Trump has held as a “negotiating chip” after December’s $11 billion tranche stayed on hold through dinner. Friday’s programme is private tea in the Red Room with Melania Trump and Peng Liyuan, then the National Archives for the Declaration, the Constitution and the Bill of Rights before Xi departs. A summit that extends the trade truce to 10 January 2027 before the first formal toast and leaves the arms pause untouched through the banquet is a summit that has priced the dinner and still has not priced the invoice.

### Hormuz’s clock meets a sequencing fight

*Rezaei’s four-to-five-day window from Wednesday points at Sunday or Monday. New York is talking phases. Tehran says the blockade lifts first.*

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, told state television on Wednesday that Washington has four to five days to accept Iran’s seven conditions — until then, no negotiations and no reopening of Hormuz. By Friday morning that clock runs toward the weekend. Reuters reported Thursday that U.S. and Iranian negotiators in New York are exploring a phased path that would reopen the strait against a lift of the U.S. economic blockade; Iranian, regional and Western sources said the obstacle is who moves first. The National’s Friday reporting, citing Iranian and Western sources, said Tehran hardened: the talks were “workshops” to exchange positions, Iran offered no concession from its public line, and Rezaei repeated that Hormuz stays closed until the conditions are met. About eighty countries backed a Bahrain-read UN statement demanding the strait reopen; Europe and Washington are drafting a separate Security Council text for an international maritime mission Iran opposes. A diplomacy that prices barrels in phases and ultimata in days is still a dispute over sequence, not a ceasefire.

## WEST ASIA — Breaking / Energy

By Aarav

### Bessent’s teens meet the shuttle’s six. The fuse is still short.

*Treasury counts 10 to 17 million barrels a day through Hormuz. AP’s dark southern corridor is still about 6, plus about 2 at Fujairah.*

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Wednesday that American forces were helping move between 10 million and 17 million barrels of oil a day through the Strait of Hormuz, against about 20 million before the war, with roughly 3 million on alternative routes. Associated Press reporting on Gulf workarounds, still current into Thursday, puts the dark southern shuttle at about 6 million barrels a day or more, with the Fujairah pipeline adding about 2 million — leaving a physical gap against Bessent’s upper teens even after inventory draws and demand destruction. CENTCOM said it had redirected 115 commercial vessels by 23 September while enforcing the blockade of Iranian ports. Bessent’s secondary aviation pressure — fuel, landing and ticket cutoffs aimed at firms that serve Iranian carriers — continued to bite; Baghdad and Muscat routes were among those cancelled from around 23 September. Pezeshkian told Fox News on Thursday that “It’s America that must choose whether it wants to end this or not.” A strait Washington counts in the teens, reporters count nearer eight restored, and Tehran still treats as closed is not a midterm calendar. It is arithmetic under a weekend clock.

## SINO-ASIA — Politics

By Aarav

### The Archives is the communiqué stage

*Xi used “oppose” and “adhere.” The $14 billion pause is what allies will read as the baseline.*

Friday’s National Archives visit is the last public frame of Xi’s first U.S. state visit in more than a decade. Reuters sources had flagged that Beijing might press its reading of the 1982 communiqué on Taiwan arms sales in that setting. Thursday already delivered the ask in the Oval: prudence on Taiwan and opposition to independence, per Chinese state media. Senate Democrats criticised Trump for failing to address China’s military pressure on Taiwan and called for immediate delivery of approved packages. Craig Singleton at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies told Reuters that Xi’s “adhere” implies Beijing believes Washington is already behaving as China wants, and that the arms standdown risks becoming a baseline rather than a pause. An ally that watched the $14 billion invoice stay dark through the East Room dinner will not wait for the Archives photograph to decide what the baseline now is.

## USA — Politics

By Aarav

### The Senate kept the war’s authorisation fog

*Thursday’s resolution demanding an end to the Iran war or a fresh authorisation failed. Midterms are six weeks out.*

On Thursday the U.S. Senate narrowly rejected a resolution demanding that President Trump end the war in Iran or seek congressional authorisation, The National reported, as Democrats tried to put Republicans on the defensive less than six weeks before the 3 November midterms. Polls have shown Republicans struggling to keep slim majorities; more of them have edged away from the conflict as petrol and diesel costs bite voters. The vote does not reopen Hormuz and does not rewrite Bessent’s secondary sanctions. It does put a date on the domestic fuse running beside Rezaei’s. A chamber that will not force the authorisation question six weeks before an election is a chamber that has priced the war as someone else’s calendar until November.
