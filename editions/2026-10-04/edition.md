## FRONT

By Riya

### Brazil votes. Half is the only number that ends it.

*First round today. About 160 million can cast a ballot. A runoff is already on the calendar for 25 October.*

Brazilians vote Sunday for a president, governors, two-thirds of the Senate and the entire lower house — the world’s fourth-largest democracy on a single day. The Economist’s tracker still frames the race as Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers’ Party against Flávio Bolsonaro, a Liberal Party senator and son of Jair Bolsonaro, who remains under house arrest for the post-2022 coup attempt. Bloomberg’s late-September polling had them deadlocked into the final stretch; neither side has locked a clear path above half of valid votes, which is the only print that skips a second round. The next president can appoint four of eleven Supreme Court justices. Trump’s tariff pressure on Brazilian imports and a streak of right-wing wins across Latin America since January 2025 sit in the same tracker. Polls open and close on Brasília time; the question Sunday answers is whether the first round ends or merely narrows.

### Day seven under the barrel. The committee sits this week.

*Nayara’s ₹5 and ₹3 still stand. Cash stays dark until Monday. Repo is 5.25 percent.*

Nayara Energy’s Saturday lift — petrol up ₹5 a litre and diesel up ₹3 across 7,108 stations — remains the clearest Indian pump print of the weekend barrel; state-owned Indian Oil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum have largely held, and Jio-bp’s network has not moved in the same write-ups. Indian cash, F&O, currency and commodity markets stay shut through the holiday hangover and reopen Monday, 5 October. The RBI Monetary Policy Committee meets 5–7 October with the policy repo at 5.25 percent; a PTI poll of sixteen economists and bankers, carried by the Economic Times on Sunday, expects a 0.25 percentage-point hike and a hawkish tone as West Asia risk and crude above $100 feed inflation forecasts. The Wall Street Journal reports Saudi Arabia has resumed loadings on the repaired East-West pipeline toward Yanbu — a Red Sea bypass that takes some barrels off the Hormuz ledger without retiring the risk premium. The settles that matter are Monday’s open and the committee’s statement midweek.

## INDIA — Politics

By Aarav

### Second day at Jantar. About 150 more detained.

*Yogendra Yadav, Kapil Sibal, Prashant Bhushan among those held. Three FIRs from Friday still stand.*

For a second consecutive day, Delhi Police stopped marchers demanding Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s resignation over the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls from reaching Jantar Mantar. The Express’s Sunday update puts Saturday’s detention count around 150, including AISA national president Neha Bora, Yogendra Yadav and senior advocate Prashant Bhushan; Friday’s tally in the same paper sat around 550. Prohibitory orders remain in the New Delhi district; police say permission was never granted. Three FIRs registered Saturday against unidentified people over Friday’s blocked gathering — two at Parliament Street, one at Connaught Place — invoke Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita sections on disobedience, obstruction, criminal force against a public servant, and common intention. Rahul Gandhi publicly pressed Union Home Minister Amit Shah over alleged misconduct toward women at the cordon. The street count and the SIR map are still the same week in two registers; Uttarakhand’s final roll, published Saturday, remains about 8.02 lakh short of the pre-SIR electorate.

## USA — Economy / Politics

By Aarav

### The wallet is still the midterm. Five weeks left.

*Economic approval near 26 percent in the AP-NORC print circulating with the coverage. Election day is 3 November.*

President Trump told reporters the economy has a “public relations” problem — that the country is “doing better than any country in the world economically” and that what is missing is the explanation. The New York Times frames the same week as a president who hoped to tout growth on the trail and is instead fighting high prices and slow hiring with five weeks to the midterms. An AP-NORC poll circulating with that coverage puts approval of his economic handling near 26 percent and cost-of-living handling near 17 percent; about 65 percent blame his policies for persistently high costs. The household print of the barrel is still at the pump. The campaign clock that is not Hormuz is 3 November.

## EUROPE — Diplomacy / Energy

By Aarav

### Lukoil’s OFAC clock still runs to 29 October.

*About $20 billion of international assets. Treasury still holds the pen.*

President Vladimir Putin’s September raise of a Lukoil international-asset sale with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff — oil fields, European refineries that make diesel and jet fuel, and gas stations including in New Jersey — remains the Europe energy file without a fresh weekend close. Lukoil had valued the portfolio at about $20 billion earlier this year; Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control must still approve, and the latest sanctions-deadline extension runs through 29 October. This desk stays on the Ukraine channel and the OFAC date — not a second Hormuz column.
