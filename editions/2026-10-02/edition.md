## FRONT

By Riya

### Day five under the barrel. Markets dark. Brent at one-oh-two.

*Sensex closed 71,909.70. Nifty 22,421.95. Eighth weekly loss locked. Cash shut for Gandhi Jayanti.*

Thursday locked the eighth consecutive weekly decline — the longest since 2001. The Sensex finished 570.59 points, or 0.79 percent, lower at 71,909.70; the Nifty fell 198.50 points, or 0.88 percent, to 22,421.95. Auto led the sector damage; breadth was 2,574 declines against 1,023 advances on the NSE. Foreign portfolio investors have now sold more than ₹3.05 lakh crore of Indian equities in the first nine months of 2026 — a record annual outflow with a quarter still to run — and close to ₹3.58 lakh crore including Fully Accessible Route bonds. Brent was near $102.58 and West Texas Intermediate near $92.91 on Friday as Economic Times tracked a third straight session higher: China has not cleared October liquid-fuel exports beyond Hong Kong and Macau, and the Wall Street Journal reported the Pentagon is sending a third carrier strike group plus 9,000 to 10,000 more troops toward the Gulf by the end of November, with President Trump weighing renewed strikes after the 3 November midterms. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told European allies Thursday to release strategic diesel reserves “immediately”; U.S. retail diesel averages about $6.39 a gallon, up more than 70 percent since the Iran war began. Indian cash, F&O, currency and commodity markets are shut Friday for Gandhi Jayanti and reopen Monday, 5 October. The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee still meets 5–7 October.

### Greer: the deal is in the short strokes. It is not imminent.

*Sticking points named. Another Modi–Trump call “very soon.” Goyal met him in Milwaukee.*

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told reporters after the G20 trade ministers’ meeting in Milwaukee that India–U.S. talks are “toward the end” — “short strokes,” in his phrase — but that he does not think “something is imminent.” The two sides have identified “the universe of items that are sticking points.” Greer said he had a “very constructive conversation” with Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal on the sidelines Wednesday and that staff are meeting while both delegations are in the country. President Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi spoke Wednesday morning U.S. time; Greer’s sense is they may speak again “very soon” to assess progress. Goyal had said on 24 September the pact was “done and dusted” pending competitive access in the American market. The interim bilateral trade agreement framework dates to February. The sticking-point list is what remains on the page.

## INDIA — Politics

By Aarav

### Five hundred detained for asking the CEC to go.

*Atishi, Bharadwaj, Yadav, Bora among those picked up near Jantar Mantar. Permission refused Thursday.*

Delhi Police detained about 500 people on Friday as protesters tried to gather near Jantar Mantar to demand the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Former Delhi chief minister Atishi, AAP Delhi chief Saurabh Bharadwaj, activist Yogendra Yadav and AISA president Neha Bora were among those taken away in buses toward Badarpur and Outer North stations. Police had refused permission for the demonstration on Thursday over alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls and had tightened Central Delhi security. Groups of ten or more were moved on; a smaller knot remained around Janpath. The SIR fight that took Odisha’s CEO to VRS this week is now a street detention count in the capital.

## WEST ASIA — Energy

By Aarav

### Flows recovered. The drones did not stop.

*NYT: weekly attacks on commercial ships in Hormuz. Many miss. The risk premium stays.*

Gulf crude exports have moved back toward prewar levels, The New York Times reports, even as Iran keeps launching drones and missiles at commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz each week — often missing, according to a Western security official. The Front owns the barrel, the carrier, and the diesel ask. This is the physical delta: recovered flow with an attack tempo that has not closed.

## SINO-ASIA — Technology / Politics

By Aarav

### China built the habit. Now it wants the limit.

*Entertainment, relationships, farming advice. Beijing steps in on dependence and minors.*

China’s drive to put artificial intelligence in daily life has produced a second problem: too much usage. The New York Times reports citizens leaning on the tools for entertainment, relationships and even farming advice, while the government steps up rules aimed at unhealthy dependence, self-harm risks, and bans on virtual relationships between chatbots and minors. The build-out is no longer only a capacity story. The usage curve is the one Beijing is trying to bend.

[New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/02/world/asia/china-ai-overuse.html)

## USA — Economy / Politics

By Aarav

### A thousand-dollar fill-up. The midterms are five weeks out.

*Truckers stretch routes or park rigs as diesel leads the affordability fight.*

American truckers are among the hardest hit by diesel that can push a single fill-up past $1,000, The New York Times reports — some staying on the road for months, others parking. Average U.S. diesel near $6.39 a gallon is the same number Washington is waving at Europe’s strategic reserves. Midterms remain 3 November. The fill-up is the household print of the barrel on Front.

[New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/02/us/truckers-diesel-costs.html)
